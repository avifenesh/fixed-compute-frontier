# Feature-DAG matrix replacement — Stage 0 decision

Status: **reject; no candidate admitted and no GPU justified**  
Date: 2026-07-26  
Execution: CPU only, CUDA hidden and reported unavailable

## Decision

The fixed feature DAG demonstrates the narrow circuit-complexity result but
fails as a replacement for dense QKV/O and FFN projections.

It won all five hierarchy worlds and represented normalized prefix sums nearly
exactly.  Adding only a 25% independently rotated dense component was already
fatal: it missed the frozen mixed noninferiority gate in all five worlds.  Its
Haar error was almost the same as the lookup control and 58.86 times the dense
linear error at the median.

The branch is therefore rejected rather than repaired with more stages,
learned routing, periodic dense layers, or a different topology.  Those are
different mechanisms and require a return to zero with their own reason and
frozen controls.

## Frozen result

All variants used exactly 1,024 learned scalars.  There were 120 fits: five
worlds, four target families, and six variants.  Each fit used its own
validation-selected checkpoint after the preregistered 1,200-step budget.

| hierarchical | 0.13795 | 0.36278 | 0.35875 | 0.16895 | 0.88998 | **0.04320** |

Values are median test NRMSE over five worlds; lower is better.

Frozen gates:

- prefix NRMSE at most 0.05: **pass**;
- hierarchy wins in at least four worlds: **pass, 5/5**;
- hierarchy relative improvement at least 10%: **pass, 69.74% median**;
- mixed within 5% of the strongest control in at least four worlds:
  **fail, 0/5**;
- Haar within 10% of dense linear: **fail, 58.86x median error**.

The mixed feature-DAG error was between 1.94 and 2.87 times the strongest
control in every world.  This is not an optimization tie or one-world failure.

## What was proved

The inclusive-prefix matrix is full rank and contains 528 nonzero terms at
`D=32`.  The executable five-stage parallel scan reproduces it to
`1.33e-15` maximum absolute error using 129 additions; the serial recurrence
uses 31.  Full rank therefore does not imply quadratic circuit work.

The learned screen also shows that a matched-topology nonlinear DAG can exploit
this bias: it beat every equal-scalar control on all five hierarchy worlds.
This is a mechanism-positive control, not evidence that language-model
projections possess the same ancestry.

## Why the architecture claim fails

A generic matrix spends its parameters on independent directions.  The fixed
DAG instead spends them on repeated local ancestry.  Its advantage is large
only when the target is generated by that ancestry.  The mixed target shows
that the two are not smoothly interchangeable: a small dense component erased
the structured advantage before hardware costs were considered.

The feature DAG also did not remove active parameter traffic in this gate.  It
read all 1,024 learned scalars per example, used 832 multiplications, 672
additions, and 160 `tanh` evaluations.  Dense linear used 1,024
multiplications and 992 additions with no transcendental operations.  Logical
arithmetic therefore supplies no physical-speed presumption on H100/H200.

Lookup was the only control reading a conditional subset: 128 parameter
scalars, four table-vector reads, 128 executed additions, 12 comparisons, and
120 runtime fixed-buffer bytes.  Its NRMSE near 0.88 on hierarchy and Haar
shows that conditional reads alone do not preserve continuous feature mixing.

## Retained boundary

Retain one statement only: **projection cost is governed by learned circuit
complexity, not matrix rank**.  Reopen this family only after independent
evidence that real jointly trained QKV/FFN transforms have reusable
subexpressions that survive a dense rotated component.  Do not infer that from
a teacher generated by the same DAG, and do not rent a GPU for this fixed
topology.
